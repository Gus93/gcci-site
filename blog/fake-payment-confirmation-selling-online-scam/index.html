Sold something online and the “payment confirmation” was fake? Here’s what to do

By Lisa · Founder & Lead Investigator

You listed something you no longer needed, a buyer said yes right away, and a screenshot or email arrived showing the payment had gone through. You shipped the item or handed it over. Then you checked your account and there was nothing there. Or the buyer sent too much by “mistake” and asked you to send the difference back, and now that money is gone too. If this is where you are, take a breath. This is a common, well-worn trick, and it works on careful people because the paperwork looks so convincing.

Here is how it usually works, what to do in the next few hours, and what is realistic to expect afterward.

How the fake payment trick works

The core of it is simple: you are shown proof of a payment instead of the payment itself. A screenshot of a Zelle or Venmo transfer, a forwarded email that looks like it came from PayPal, or a text claiming “funds are pending until you ship” are all easy to fake and cost the scammer nothing. Real payment apps do not ask a seller to ship first so that money can be released, and a payment that has really arrived shows up in your own account, not in a message the buyer sends you.

The overpayment version adds a second step. The buyer “accidentally” pays more than the asking price, by check or a payment that will later bounce or be reversed, and asks you to refund the extra. You send real money back, and the original payment disappears. It is the same idea as the one we describe in our guide on what to do if you deposited a fake check, just moved into a payment app.

What to do right now

Start by confirming the facts in your own account, using the bank or app directly, never a link from the buyer. If the money truly is not there, stop all contact about payment and do not send anything else, including a “refund” of an overpayment, shipping fees, or a fee to “unlock” the funds.

If you have not shipped yet, you have lost nothing. Cancel the sale and keep the messages. If you have shipped, contact the carrier right away and ask whether the package can be stopped or redirected before delivery; this sometimes works, particularly with a tracking number that has not yet reached the buyer. If you handed the item over in person, write down everything you remember about the person, the vehicle, and where you met, while it is fresh.

If you sent money back after an overpayment, call your bank or the app’s support line immediately. Speed matters most with peer-to-peer apps, and our guide on what to do if you sent money by Zelle walks through what to ask for, even though recovery is never guaranteed.

Save everything before it disappears

Scammers delete accounts and listings quickly. Before that happens, screenshot the buyer’s profile, the listing, the whole conversation, the fake confirmation, and the shipping receipt and tracking page. Save any email in its original form rather than a forward, since the sender details are what an investigator or platform can actually use. Our short piece on how to preserve evidence covers the details, and it takes about ten minutes.

Report it

Report the buyer to the marketplace where you listed, which can remove the account and sometimes flag the listing for other sellers. File a report with the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and with the FBI’s IC3, especially if you lost money or goods of real value. A local police report is also worth making when the item was expensive, because insurers and some platforms ask for a report number. Be realistic about the outcome: most of these cases do not end with the item or money returned. Reports still matter, because they are how accounts get shut down and patterns get noticed.

Next time you sell

A few habits close most of the door on this. Treat money as received only when it shows in your own account and has cleared, not when someone sends a screenshot. Be cautious with buyers who are in a hurry, offer more than you asked, or insist on a courier you did not choose. For local sales, meet somewhere public, and let the buyer pay in front of you while you watch the transfer land. If you are also buying on these platforms, our post on how to tell if a Marketplace seller is a scammer covers the mirror image of this.

Trusting a buyer was not a failure of judgment. The scam is built so that a normal, polite sale looks exactly like a fraudulent one until the money fails to show up. If you would like a second set of eyes on what you have, text or email us and we will tell you plainly what we see.
