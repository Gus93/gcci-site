Fake copyright violation message on Instagram or Facebook

By Lisa · Founder & Lead Investigator

A message lands in your inbox or direct messages: your Instagram or Facebook account has been flagged for copyright infringement, you have 24 hours to appeal, or the page will be permanently disabled. There is a button to click and a form to fill in. Your stomach drops, especially if that account holds years of photos or runs your business. Take a breath. These notices are a well-worn phishing tactic, and the pressure you feel is exactly what they are designed to produce.

How the fake copyright notice works

The message usually claims to come from “Meta Support,” “Instagram Copyright,” or a “Page Policy Team.” It may arrive as an email, a DM from an account with an official-looking logo, or a comment tagging you. The wording is vague about what you supposedly copied, because there is no real claim. The link leads to a page that looks like a login or appeal form and asks for your password, a two-factor code, or sometimes a payment to “verify” the account. Once you enter them, the scammer logs in as you, changes the email and phone number, and locks you out. Hijacked accounts are then often used to message your followers or run ads and sales scams in your name.

Signs it isn’t real

A few details tend to give it away. Genuine platform notices can be found inside the app itself, in your account status or support inbox, so if the only place you can see the warning is a message or email, that is a red flag. The link may use a lookalike address, a link shortener, or a domain that is not the platform’s own. The message threatens a deadline and a permanent ban, and asks for login details, which a real support team does not request. You may also see odd grammar or a sender address that does not match the company. When in doubt, don’t click anything in the message. Open the app or type the site address yourself and look there.

If you haven’t clicked yet

Good. Don’t reply or tap the link, and leave the message alone for now. Take a screenshot if you like, then report it as spam or phishing inside the platform and delete it. Check your account status through the app’s own settings, and turn on two-factor authentication using an authenticator app rather than text messages if that option is available. It is worth a minute to confirm which devices are currently logged in.

If you entered your password or a code

Move fast, but calmly. Go directly to the official app or site and change your password to a new, unique one. Sign out of all other sessions, and check that the email address and phone number on the account are still yours. If you reused that password anywhere else, change it there too, starting with your email. Our guides on what to do after clicking a phishing link and on recovering a hacked social media account walk through the steps in order. If you are already locked out, use the platform’s official account recovery flow and look out for fake “recovery helpers,” who are a second scam aimed at people in your position.

Warn your followers, and watch for impersonation

If the account was taken over, tell friends and followers through another channel that any messages from it should be ignored, especially ones asking for money or codes. Scammers sometimes copy a profile instead of taking it over, and our post on someone impersonating you on social media explains how to report that. Keep screenshots of the fake notice, the link, and any messages sent from your account, since they help if you need to file a report or support request.

Reporting it

Report the message to the platform from within the app, and you can file a report with the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If you paid anything to “verify” the account, contact your card issuer or payment app right away and tell them exactly what happened.

Falling for a convincing, urgent message says nothing bad about you. These notices are built by people who do this all day. If your account holds something you can’t afford to lose, or you are stuck in a recovery loop, you are welcome to reach out to us privately and we can talk it through.
