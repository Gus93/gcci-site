What to do after a gold bar courier scam

A caller told you your accounts had been hacked, or that your identity was tied up in a federal case, and the only safe move was to convert your savings into gold. Then someone came to your door, or met you in a parking lot, and took the bars. If that is where you are right now, the panic you feel is exactly what the scam was built to create. Let us slow it down and walk through what actually helps.

First, this was not a lapse in judgment. The gold bar courier scam is run by organized crews who rehearse these calls, hand victims off between a fake “bank investigator,” a fake “federal agent,” and a courier, and keep the pressure on for days. The FBI has tracked the surge closely: from 2023 through May 2025 it documented more than 1,700 cases of couriers collecting cash or gold, with losses near $186 million, and roughly 98 percent of the money was lost by people over 60. You are not the first person this happened to, and being targeted says nothing about you.

The one rule that unwinds the whole scam

No real government agency, bank, or law enforcement officer will ever tell you to buy gold, withdraw cash, or hand valuables to a courier to keep them “safe.” That request, by itself, is the proof it is a scam. So if the calls are still coming, stop answering. If they claim more gold is needed to “clear” your case or release your funds, do not buy it. There is no second payment that fixes the first one.

Stop the money that is still yours

Call your bank and any brokerage you drew from, tell them plainly that you were defrauded, and ask them to flag the accounts and stop any pending transfers or withdrawals. If the scammers set up recurring moves or opened anything in your name, this is the call that closes those doors. Change the passwords and PINs on your financial and email accounts from a device the scammers never touched, since these crews often talk victims into installing remote-access software along the way.

Report it today, while the trail is fresh

Filing quickly matters, because couriers and drop addresses can sometimes be traced when the report lands early. Do these today:

  • File with the FBI at IC3.gov. Include dates, dollar amounts, the courier’s description, any “code word” you were given, and phone numbers used.
  • Report to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  • Call your local police and ask for a report and a case number. Banks and investigators often need that reference before they will act.
  • If you shared a Social Security number or other identity details, start a recovery plan at IdentityTheft.gov.

Save the evidence before it disappears

Write down everything you remember while it is fresh: what the callers said, the order they called in, the name and story the courier gave, and where the handoff happened. Keep the phone numbers, any texts or emails, receipts from the gold purchase, and bank withdrawal slips together in one place. If a courier came to your home, your doorbell or security camera footage can be genuinely useful, so save it now before it is overwritten. Our guide to preserving evidence in the first 24 hours covers how to do this without accidentally destroying anything.

Expect a second scam, and turn it away

Within days you may hear from someone claiming they can recover your gold or your money for an upfront fee, sometimes posing as a government “asset recovery” office. It is the same playbook aimed at people who were just hit. Legitimate help is not sold as a guaranteed refund, and no honest party asks to be paid in gift cards, wire transfers, or crypto to get your money back.

When to bring in an investigator

For a large loss, a documented workup can turn your report into something law enforcement or a court can actually use, tracing phone numbers, courier logistics, and any payment rails involved. Our Investigation Help page explains the smaller cases we sometimes take on, and for five and six figure losses that need court-grade attribution we point people to Rexxfield.

Nothing here guarantees the gold comes back. What acting quickly protects is the trail, and your accounts, and your footing, and those are worth defending while the scammers are still counting on your silence.
