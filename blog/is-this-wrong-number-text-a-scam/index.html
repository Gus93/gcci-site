Is this “wrong number” text the start of a scam?

A text lands from a number you don’t recognize. “Hey, are we still on for dinner Thursday?” Maybe it’s “Is this Dr. Lee’s office?” or just a photo of someone smiling on a hiking trail. You weren’t planning to talk to anyone new today, so you write back to say they’ve reached the wrong person. They’re warm about the mix-up. The conversation keeps going. That small, harmless-looking exchange is how one of the most expensive scams of the last few years usually begins.

If you’re reading this because a thread like that is open on your phone right now, you’re asking the right question at the right time. Most people don’t think to check until money is already gone.

Why a total stranger is texting you by “accident”

These messages are not misfires. Criminal groups send them by the thousands, often from scam compounds overseas, using software that blasts random numbers and waits to see who answers kindly. A polite reply tells them two useful things: the number is real, and the person behind it is willing to talk to a stranger. That is all they need to start. Investigators call the larger scheme pig butchering, a blunt name for a slow process where someone is “fattened up” with attention and trust before the money gets taken.

So is the wrong number text itself the scam?

On its own, a single misdirected text is just noise. The scam is what comes after you answer. The person on the other end is in no hurry. They’ll chat for days or weeks about ordinary things, your job, your dog, your weekend plans. Somewhere in there they mention they’ve done well trading crypto, or an uncle who taught them how, and they offer to show you. By the time an app or a website gets named, you don’t feel like you’re talking to a stranger anymore. That feeling is the product, and it was built on purpose.

You already replied. What now?

Nothing bad happens from one reply by itself. You haven’t lost anything by saying “wrong number.” The move now is simply to stop. Don’t keep the conversation alive out of politeness, and don’t explain yourself. Block the number and delete the thread. If the chat already jumped to WhatsApp, Telegram, or a dating app, block it there too. You don’t owe a friendly goodbye to a script.

How to tell a real wrong number from bait

An honest misfire ends quickly. A real person says sorry and moves on the moment you tell them they’ve got the wrong number. Bait does the opposite. It lingers. It asks you questions about yourself, nudges toward friendship, and sounds a little too polished and a little too available. If a stranger who texted you by “mistake” is still messaging a week later, and the topic has drifted anywhere near money, investing, or some opportunity you’d be foolish to pass up, you already have your answer.

If it already turned into an investment

If you’ve put money into a platform this person showed you, that’s a more urgent situation, and you are not foolish for getting there. These scripts are written by professionals to work on careful, intelligent people. Save everything first. Screenshot the chats, the person’s profile, the website or app, and any wallet addresses or transfers. Our guide to preserving evidence in the first 24 hours covers what matters most. Then report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to the FBI at IC3.gov. If that platform is now demanding a “tax” or “fee” before it will release your balance, that fee is part of the scam, and you should stop there.

Crypto doesn’t reverse, but it does leave a trail on a public ledger, so where the money went can often be followed even when it can’t be clawed straight back. For a larger loss where you want that traced properly, our Investigation Help page covers the smaller cases we take on, and we route court-grade attribution to Rexxfield. For a text you haven’t answered yet, the whole thing is far simpler. Don’t reply. Block it. The cheapest day to walk away from this is today.
