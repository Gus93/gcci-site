Gaming account scam: what parents can do next

Your child comes to you upset. Their game account is gone, or the skins and in-game items they spent months collecting have vanished, or a payment shows up on your card that nobody agreed to. Often the story starts the same way: someone in a chat offered free currency, a trade that sounded too good to pass up, or help with a problem, and a link or a login code changed hands.

If that has happened in your home, take a breath. Gaming scams are built to work on smart, careful kids, and on adults too. They rely on excitement and trust, not on carelessness. Here is how they usually work, what to do first, and how to keep the conversation with your child a calm one.

How these scams usually work

Most fall into a few patterns. A “free currency” or “free skins” offer sends your child to a lookalike website that asks them to log in, which hands over the password. A trade offer pressures them to move first, then the other player disappears. Someone posing as a moderator, a friend, or game support says the account is about to be banned and asks for a verification code. Occasionally the pattern is an older player who builds friendship over weeks and then asks for gift cards, photos, or a switch to a private chat.

That last pattern deserves its own attention. If anyone has asked your child for pictures or threatened them, please read our guide on financial sextortion of teens before you do anything else.

What to do in the first hour

Start with the account. If your child can still sign in, change the password right away, turn on two-step verification using an authenticator app rather than text messages if the platform allows it, and sign out of all other devices. Do the same for the email address linked to the game, since whoever controls the email can take back the account. If the login no longer works, use the platform’s official recovery page, never a link someone sent in a message. Our guide on what to do if your social media account was hacked follows nearly the same steps.

Next, look at the money. Check the card, app store account, and any linked payment methods for charges you do not recognize. Contact the card issuer about unauthorized charges, and ask the platform about a refund request, since many have a process for purchases made without a parent’s permission or through a compromised account. Remove saved cards from the account while you sort it out.

Talk with your child first, not last

The most useful thing you can do is make it easy for your child to tell you what really happened. Kids often hide these incidents because they fear losing their gaming time or being blamed. If they believe telling you leads to punishment, the next scam, or the next request from a stranger, will stay secret too. Say plainly that they are not in trouble, that you only want to understand what was said and what was shared, and that adults get fooled by these tricks every day.

If your child is being teased or targeted by other players, as opposed to scammed, that is a different problem with its own steps, covered in what parents can do about cyberbullying.

Save evidence and report it

Before anything is deleted, take screenshots of the chat, the usernames and profile links of the people involved, the fake website address, and any payment receipts. Report the accounts inside the game or chat platform, since that is the fastest way to get them removed. Then file a report with the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and with IC3.gov if real money was lost. Even when it feels like nothing happens, those reports are how patterns get noticed.

Helping it not happen again

A few household rules go a long way, and they work best when your child helps write them. Never share a password or a verification code with anyone, including someone claiming to be support. Real game support does not ask for them. Treat any offer of free currency as a trap. Use a different password for each account, and consider turning on spending limits or parental approval for purchases. Most of all, agree that a strange request in a chat is something you can talk about together, without anyone losing privileges for mentioning it.

A stolen game account can feel small to adults and enormous to a kid. Taking it seriously, and staying calm, is what keeps your child coming to you the next time something feels off.
