Someone is using your SSN to work: what to do

The letter looks routine until you read the numbers. The IRS says you earned wages from an employer you have never heard of, or your tax return is questioned because income “doesn’t match.” Or you check your Social Security earnings record and find a year of work you never did. Someone, somewhere, has a job under your Social Security number.

That is employment-related identity theft. It is different from most identity theft because the thief usually isn’t trying to take your money. They want to work, often because they can’t get a number of their own. The practical effect is still real: extra income gets reported under your name, and that can trigger tax notices, confuse your benefits record, and sometimes cause an employer or agency to ask questions. It is fixable, and you are not in trouble for income you didn’t earn.

How people usually find out

Most people learn from an IRS notice saying wages or income were reported that aren’t on your return. Others find it by reviewing their Social Security statement, being told by a state agency, or having a benefit application questioned. The number may have come from a data breach, stolen mail, or a purchased list; you did nothing wrong. If a company recently told you your data was exposed, our guide to what to do after a data breach notification letter covers the broader lockdown.

Confirm what is actually on your record

Before you respond to anything, check the facts. Create or log in to your free my Social Security account at ssa.gov and review your earnings record year by year. Pull your IRS wage and income transcript through your IRS online account, which lists every W-2 filed under your number and the employer behind it. Write down the employer name, the tax year, and the amount for each entry that isn’t yours.

Use the contact details on the official IRS or SSA sites, not a number printed in an unexpected message. Scammers sometimes imitate these agencies, so if a notice arrives by text or email, treat it with suspicion and go to the website directly.

Tell the IRS, in writing

If an IRS notice cites income that isn’t yours, respond by the date on the notice, even if the amount seems wrong. Explain that you did not work for that employer and that you believe someone is using your Social Security number. The IRS offers an Identity Theft Affidavit, Form 14039, for exactly this situation; its page on employment-related identity theft explains how it works. Keep copies of everything you send and note the date.

Also file a report at IdentityTheft.gov. It produces an FTC Identity Theft Report and a recovery plan that other agencies and employers will recognize.

Let Social Security know

Contact the Social Security Administration about the earnings that aren’t yours. The SSA explains what to do if someone is misusing your number on its identity theft FAQ. In most cases the SSA does not hand out a new number, because that can create new problems, but it can help correct your earnings record so your future benefits are calculated on what you really earned.

Lock down the rest of your credit

If a thief had your number for work, assume they could have used it elsewhere. A credit freeze is free and takes minutes at each bureau; see how to freeze your credit. Then review your reports at AnnualCreditReport.com for accounts you don’t recognize, and consider an IRS Identity Protection PIN to prevent someone from filing a return in your name. If that has already happened, our guide to what to do when someone files a tax return in your name is the next read.

Expect it to take time, and keep a folder

The IRS can be slow to reconcile these cases, sometimes months. That is frustrating but normal. Keep one folder with every notice, letter, and confirmation number, and follow up politely. Check your earnings record again after a few months, because corrections don’t always show up right away.

You are not responsible for a stranger’s paycheck, and you are allowed to insist your record say so. If the same person keeps using your number, or you suspect someone you know, that is where a private investigation can help establish the facts. Most people resolve this with the steps above and some patience.
